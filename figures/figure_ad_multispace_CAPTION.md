# Figure caption — multi-space applicability domain

**Figure X. An applicability-domain verdict is a property of the library *and*
the descriptor space, not of the library alone.**

The same rule was applied in all eight spaces — a compound is in-domain if its
mean Tanimoto (or, for the physicochemical space, cosine) similarity to its
five nearest neighbours in the ChEMBL A2AR training set (n = 5,970) reaches the
10th percentile of that training set's own self-similarity. No threshold was
tuned and no space was preferred a priori.

**(A)** Coverage before and after similarity selection. The 236-compound
SNP-Lib (grey) was expanded to 497 compounds by selecting on 2D pharmacophore
and 3D shape similarity to 52 known A2AR ligands and then keeping the top 500
by docking score (coloured; three compounds failed to dock). That deliberate
pull toward known ligands raised physicochemical coverage from 64.4 % to
86.3 %, while coverage in ECFP4, FCFP4, topological-torsion and Gobbi 2D
pharmacophore space stayed at exactly zero. Structural and physicochemical
similarity are decoupled: satisfying one says nothing about the other.

**(B)** The full similarity distribution in each space against that space's
threshold (red line); violins show the 497 redocked compounds, bars the
interquartile range, white points the median. The structural spaces are not
marginally outside the domain: in ECFP4 the median compound reaches 0.241 and
even the single most ChEMBL-like compound of all 497 reaches only 0.352,
against a threshold of 0.588.

**(C)** Number of the eight spaces that call each compound in-domain. No
compound is inside more than two; 52 are inside none, and 429 of the 494
in-domain calls come from the physicochemical space. A study that reports a
single applicability-domain figure is therefore reporting its choice of
descriptor space at least as much as it is reporting its library.
